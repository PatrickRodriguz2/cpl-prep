Exam-faithful mocks, scored the way the examiner scores

100 questions across air law, navigation, meteorology and aeronautics general knowledge, with the full 3½-hour countdown, flagging and a question palette. No feedback until you submit, exactly like exam day. The pass rule is the one that applies: 60% overall and 60% in each of the four areas. Below 60% overall means rewriting the whole paper; 60% overall with a failed area is a partial pass, and the result names the areas you would rewrite. The study guide publishes no split of the paper by area, so the mock follows the sizes of the four supplementary papers and says that it is an estimate.

The four supplementary papers

Practise exactly what a partial pass asks for: CALAW (Air Law, 20 questions), CANAV (Navigation, 25), CAMET (Meteorology, 25) and CAGEN (General Knowledge, 35), each with its own time limit, scored at 60%. Sat together they share one sitting of at most 3½ hours, and the Exam tab explains how the time adds up.

A new flight to plan every session

Every session plans a brand-new VFR cross-country between real Canadian aerodromes — three legs, winds aloft, variation, true airspeed, fuel flow and a compass deviation card — and asks the questions computed from that log: magnetic heading, groundspeed, time en route, ETA, minimum fuel, compass heading and endurance. A weight-and-balance trainer generates a loaded aeroplane and asks for take-off weight, centre of gravity, the envelope verdict and the fix, and a weather trainer generates coherent METAR and TAF pairs to decode and fly against. Fresh numbers every time, so you learn the method, not the answers.

Worked calculations

Generated pressure and density altitude, ISA deviation, true airspeed, time, speed and distance, fuel, time zone, 1-in-60, crosswind, stall speed in a turn, glide, climb and descent problems — plus the commercial pilot's medical certificate validity under CAR 404.04 (12 months, 6 months from age 40 single-pilot with passengers or from age 60, and what private-pilot privileges outlast it) — each with the full working.

Each question carries a full explanation and its reference, and a Library card per topic holds the must-know numbers — 83 cards, always free to read.
